## 1. What the proposal asks, what is kept, and where it is changed

The proposal wants a **repeatable pipeline**: parametrise a skill as a weight-space object, edit through it, and bound the damage to general generation with measurable gates (its section 0). Its five experiments are kept, in the order of cost, with these changes. Each change is a claim about the proposal and is argued, not assumed.

1. **The proposal's E1 leaves the neuron selection unspecified, and the published edit shows it matters.** The published edit (`weight-space.md` 13.1, `results/w1_diff.json` **[measured 2026-11-04]**) is rank 1 and supported on **64 of the 3,584 intermediate neurons** of `mlp.down_proj` in layers 15 and 19. A contrastive eigenproblem (proposal 2.2) returns a direction in the 1,024-d residual space (the *output* side, `u`). It does not say which 64 columns to subtract it from (the *input* side, `v`). Section 6 pre-registers the candidate rules. One more fact about the published columns, **[read from `results/w1_diff.json`, 2026-11-06]**: they are far from uniform over the neuron index. The median column is 364 (layer 15) and 353.5 (layer 19) of 3,584, and 37 and 34 of the 64 are below 500, where a uniform draw gives 8.9. Whatever rule chose them correlates with the index; a rule whose score does not (Spearman test, section 6.3) cannot have produced them.
2. **A null space of a full-rank covariance is a choice of cutoff, not a theorem.** The proposal's section 3.1 and 5 take `K0` (the inputs of `down_proj` over a corpus, 3,584-d) and project the edit off the span of its singular directions above `1e-3 * s[0]`. With more tokens than dimensions (here 150,000 or more against 3,584) `K0 K0^T` is full rank, so the *exact* null space is empty and everything that remains is "directions whose variance is below the cutoff". The guarantee `dW K0 = 0` holds only on the retained span and the retained span is whatever the cutoff makes it. Two further consequences, both stated before measuring: (a) `K0` must **exclude the target domain** (math in a thinking-chat form for this edit) or the projection removes exactly the part of the edit that does the work; (b) the proposal's number to report is not only "how much of `||dW||` survives" but **how much survives on the target-like text against general text** (selectivity, 5.3).
3. **A projected edit is no longer a column patch.** T11's `CHOPATCH` v1 **[read: PR #122, not merged]** stores, per tensor, the changed columns and the new bf16 bits of every row of those columns; it is a column patch because the published edit is. `dW P` is dense over all 3,584 columns, so it cannot be written in v1. It is rank 1, so it is `u` (1,024 floats) and `P v` (3,584 floats) per tensor: a v2 "rank-1 factor" patch, applied in float32, is the natural rollback artifact (proposal 3.5: store `dW` separately). **A second consequence, measured here (5.4):** adding a small dense `dW` to a bf16 weight and rounding to bf16 loses most of it (a bf16 entry has 8 bits of mantissa), so a projected edit must be applied in f32 (the engine's `f32` path, or `f16`, 10 bits) and not written back as a bf16 checkpoint, or the behavioural arm measures the rounding and not the edit.
4. **E2's "gain against collateral" needs a measure of gain that is not on this Mac.** The target metric is W1's (accuracy on GSM8K/MATH, share of generations at the token limit): hours on the L4 per point. So E2 is split: **E2-lite** (collateral of the published edit, 4) and **E2b** (null-space projection of the published edit, collateral side only, 5) are offline and run here; **E2c** (a cheap offline proxy for the gain, 7.1) and **E2d** (the behavioural arm: sweep over cutoff, alpha, rank on the L4) are later tickets. The proposal's decision rule "E2 shows gains only with large collateral" cannot fire from E2-lite alone (section 8, D2).
5. **The KL and perplexity gates need an anchor, which the proposal does not give.** `delta_KL` and `delta_ppl` are left as symbols. Section 4.3 fixes numbers, and runs three controls beside the published edit so that a number is read against something: a random edit of the same norm and support size, int8 rounding and int4 rounding of every linear layer of the model.
6. **E3's "gauge-invariant" `QK_h = W_Q W_K^T` is not invariant in this model family.** For a model with RoPE the gauge group of a head's QK is not `GL(d_head)` but the commutant of the rotations; Qwen3.5 also applies a per-head RMSNorm to q and k before the product (`qk_norm`), which makes "QK_h" a non-bilinear quantity. The proposal's E3 therefore trains its own small transformers (2 to 4 layers) where the architecture is chosen so the invariants are exact (learned absolute positions, no q/k norm), and says so (section 7.3). It does **not** claim signatures for Qwen3.5.
7. **The Gemma 3 270M control of E1 needs a download and a licence acceptance** (gated). Listed in 3.3 for approval, not downloaded.

Not changed: the proposal's accept/reject loop (2.5), its rollback (3.5), its non-goals. **What the post establishes and the proposal's list of unknowns** (the proposal's section 1; "how the direction was found" is unknown to it): still unknown to us. The authors' logs give the *diff* and the hashes (`weight-space.md` 13.1), not the procedure; everything in E1 is therefore our own search for a procedure that lands on, or near, the same artifact.

## 3. The corpus (pre-registered rules; the counts are section 11)

### 3.1 Rules
* **Local text only, nothing downloaded.** Sources are files already on this machine, listed by root in `w4_collateral.py` (`DOMAIN_ROOTS`); the manifest of the run (every file with its sha256, its domain, its split and its token count) is written to `results/w4_collateral_manifest.json`, **without the text**. Duplicates by content (md5) are dropped once, and one checkout per project is read (the many `cancho-gpu-*` worktrees are not), so a near-copy of a document cannot sit in both splits.
* **Domains** (target tokens in each split; the actual numbers are reported and a shortfall is flagged): `en_md` 24k (prose and Markdown of the repositories); `code_py` 14k, `code_rs` 14k, `code_cho` 10k (cancho and chx), `code_c` 8k (C, headers, CUDA, Metal), `code_web` 6k (JS, TS, shell); `data` 6k (JSON, YAML, TOML); `es` 16k (Spanish prose: the local books and manifestos); `it` 10k (Italian: a nineteenth-century novel in the public domain and two READMEs); `comfy_<lang>` 5k each for 12 languages (`en zh zh-TW ja ko ru fr es pt-BR tr ar fa`): the node documentation of ComfyUI (`comfyui_embedded_docs`, an installed package, one Markdown file per node and language, the same content translated: **parallel text, so a node is one unit across languages** and never straddles the two splits); and, **evaluation split only**, `math_chat` 12k: GSM8K test problems 200 to 399 (W1 uses 0 to 199) rendered as the W1 prompt in the model's thinking-on chat template followed by the gold solution (the calculator annotations removed), reported separately for the prompt tokens (`math_chat_prompt`) and the response (`math_chat_resp`). `math_chat` is the **target-adjacent** domain: the nearest text we have to where the edit is meant to act, and it is **never in `K0`**.
* **Split.** The unit is a file; a file longer than 16,384 tokens is cut into units of 16,384 so that a one-novel domain still has several units; a unit is in the **fit** split (used for `K0` and the activation scale) or the **eval** split (everything reported) by `sha256(unit id + "w4-20261106") mod 2`, so the split is a function of the seed string and the content address, not of file order. Documents are cut into windows of at most 2,048 tokens (the model's own tokenizer, no special tokens added); a window shorter than 32 tokens is dropped; at most 4 windows per document; windows are drawn at random per domain (numpy `default_rng(20261106)`) until the quota is met. **Scoring is one full forward per window** (no chunked prefill: `weight-space.md` 14.14, 14.12's defect: `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet` ignores a cached state unless the new chunk is one token). The first token of a window has no context and is not scored: a window of `T` tokens has `T - 1` scored positions.
* **Held-out means disjoint from `K0`** (the fit split) **and from the edit's own construction** (the edit was made elsewhere; here the published edit is only read). It is not "unseen by the model": all of these texts may be in the pre-training data of a model with this vocabulary.
* **What the local corpus cannot give, stated now:** no German, no Hindi, no Arabic prose beyond the ComfyUI translations, no long context (every window is at most 2,048 tokens: the proposal's "8k+ contexts" is not covered), no chain-of-thought *generated by the model* (the model's own long thinking text is where the loop lives: it arrives with the W1 cloud run and is used by E1/E2c), no poetry. The proposal's "multi-domain, multi-language" is met in breadth (11 non-English text sources in 12 languages, 5 code families, structured data, math) and **not** in depth; section 3.3 lists what to download to deepen it.

### 3.2 Why 3,584-d `K0` needs this many tokens
`K0 K0^T` has 3,584 rows. The estimate of a covariance with `N` tokens has relative noise of about `sqrt(d/N)` on its eigenvalues (**[estimate]**: a Marchenko-Pastur argument, not measured here); at `N` about 170,000 that is 14%, small against the factor 10 to 100 between the cutoffs of 5.2. So the fit split aims at 168k tokens (`N` > 40 `d`).

## 4. E2-lite: the collateral of the published edit (pre-registered)

### 4.1 What is run
The base `Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B` and the published edit (`~/models/qwen35-08b-base`, `~/models/qwen35-08b-edited`, hash-verified by `w1.py diff --w1-expect`), text tower only, **float32** weights widened from bf16 (`hf08_ref.build_model(..., "f32")`, `transformers` 5.2.0's shipped code, `attn_implementation=eager`), on the MPS device. **Variants**, each a full pass over the eval split against the base:

| name | what |
|---|---|
| `edited` | both published tensors (layers 15 and 19) |
| `e15`, `e19` | the published edit at one layer only |
| `p1e-2`, `p1e-3`, `p1e-4` | E2b: `dW P` at both layers, cutoffs of 5.2 |
| `rand0`, `rand1` | random edit of the same norm per layer: rank 1, 64 random columns carrying the published `v`'s values in random order, a random unit `u` of the published `||dW||_F`. Said before running: **a random direction is a weak control** (it lands in the residual stream's quiet directions); it is the floor of "an edit of this size", not a competitor |
| `q8` | every linear weight of the 24 layers (attention, gated-delta projections, MLP; not the embedding, the head, a norm, the convolution, `A_log`, `dt_bias`) rounded to int8, symmetric, per output row, absmax scaling (round to nearest) |
| `q4` | the same weights to int4, symmetric, groups of 32 along the input dimension, absmax / 7 scaling (round to nearest) |

`q8` and `q4` are the **anchors** (4.3): what a damage that the community accepts as small (8-bit) and as tolerable (4-bit, round to nearest, no calibration) does to the same metrics on this model and this corpus.

### 4.2 Metrics (per token, per sequence, per domain)
* `KL(base || variant)` per scored position, in nats, `sum_v p_b(v) (log p_b(v) - log p_e(v))` over the whole 248,320-token vocabulary, float32 `log_softmax` of the logits of one full forward each, in chunks of 256 positions. Reported: mean, p50, p90, p99, max, per token and per sequence (a sequence's value is the mean over its positions), overall and per domain, plus the mean's 95% interval by **bootstrap over sequences** (2,000 resamples, seed 20261106; sequences, not tokens, are resampled because tokens in a window are not independent).
* `NLL` of the actual next token under base and under the variant; **perplexity** `exp(mean NLL)`; the paired difference `d = NLL_variant - NLL_base` per position, its mean and the same bootstrap; the reported "delta ppl" is `exp(mean d) - 1`.
* top-1 agreement (the greedy token of base equals the variant's), reported, not gated.
* **Where the KL concentrates:** the share of the total KL carried by the top 1% and top 10% of positions; the mean KL by position bucket (`[1,16)`, `[16,128)`, `[128, 2048)`), by token class of the target (whitespace/newline, digits, punctuation, alphabetic), by deciles of the base's entropy; the 30 tokens (as strings, aggregated by string) with the largest total KL; and, only for the repository-text domains (`en_md`, `code_*`, `data`), the ±4 token context of the 20 positions of largest KL. The texts of the Spanish and Italian domains are not printed.
* **Repetition** (4.4) and **the activation of the edited neurons** (5.3).

### 4.3 The gates (fixed now; **[choice]**, with the reason)
A variant **passes** iff all of:
1. **KL.** The upper 95% bound of the overall mean KL is at most **0.02 nats**; the mean KL of every domain at most **0.05**; the per-token p99 at most **0.5**.
   *Reason.* A mean KL of the order of 0.01 to 0.03 nats per token is where 4-bit round-to-nearest quantisation of a small model sits in the community's practice **[estimate, from memory of llama.cpp's KL-divergence tool; not checked]**, and 4-bit is a damage people knowingly accept. 0.02 is that scale, not a derived bound. The measured `q8` and `q4` of this run are printed next to the gate; **if `q4`'s mean KL turns out more than 10 times above or below 0.02, that is reported, the gate stays as written, and the doc says that it is mis-scaled**. Domain and tail clauses stop a mean hiding a collapse in one language or in a few tokens.
2. **Perplexity.** The upper 95% bound of `exp(mean d) - 1` overall at most **2%**; every domain at most **5%**. *Reason.* The paired difference of a small edit has a much smaller variance than the NLL itself (**[estimate]** from the sizes of the other numbers; the bootstrap prints it), so 2% is far above the noise and at the size 8-bit to 4-bit quantisation costs on a small model.
3. **Repetition** (4.4): on the 30 general prompts the paired mean of `rep4` (edited minus base) at most **+0.03**, and the number of general prompts that end in a loop in the variant and not in the base at most **3**.
4. **No NaN or infinity** anywhere, and the number of scored positions equal between base and variant.
A **strict reading** is also printed (mean KL upper bound at most 0.002, perplexity at most +0.3%, no domain over 0.01): the scale of an edit that is meant to be invisible. It is not a gate.

**Pre-registered expectation for the published edit [estimate, written before measuring]:** mean KL between 0.002 and 0.05 nats, i.e. it **may or may not pass the 0.02 gate**; I cannot tell. The reasoning: two of 24 layers, each changed by a rank-1 matrix of relative norm 0.08 to 0.11 that writes into one residual direction; the edit is chosen to change behaviour on one mode (loops) and the model keeps generating fluently afterwards (the post's own samples), which bounds the average damage; a change of this size at one MLP can still move the next-token distribution by a few hundredths of a nat where its direction is read by later layers. The `rand` variants are expected well below `edited` (the floor), and `q4` above `q8` by about a factor 10 to 100 (**[estimate]**).

### 4.4 Repetition
**Prompts (50, fixed now):** 20 are the W1 GSM8K prompts 0 to 19 (thinking-on chat template, the authors' instruction, as `w1_behave.build_question` renders them: the target domain, where the base loops); 30 are the first 48 tokens of 30 eval-split documents (a seeded pick, at least two from each of `en_md`, the four code families, `data`, `es`, `it` and nine `comfy` languages): **general** prompts. **Generation:** greedy, 256 new tokens, no repetition penalty, stop at an end-of-sequence id (id 248044 or 248046) and then the continuation is shorter; one forward for the prompt, then one token at a time with the cache (the pattern `weight-space.md` 14.12 verified). Variants: `base`, `edited`, and the projected edit at the proposal's own cutoff, `p1e-3`.
**Metrics, on the generated tokens `g` of length `n`:** `rep4 = 1 - (distinct 4-grams) / (n - 3)` (a continuation of fewer than 36 tokens is not scored and is counted); **tail loop**: there is a period `p`, `1 <= p <= 64`, such that the last `max(32, 4p)` tokens are `p`-periodic (the W0 detector's definition of `weight-space.md` 5.1, in tokens; a planted loop and a planted non-loop are tested). Reported per group (general, math), mean `rep4`, the number of tail loops and the number of paired prompts where the variant loops and the base does not (and the reverse), with the exact list. **A 256-token greedy continuation is a weak probe of a loop that appears after thousands of tokens**; the number is a gate on "did we make general text worse", not a measure of the edit's benefit.

## 5. E2b: the null-space projection of the published edit (pre-registered)

### 5.1 The object
For each of the two tensors, `dW = W_edited - W_base` (float32, from the bf16 values), `[1024, 3584]`, rank 1 to the bf16 noise (`weight-space.md` 13.1): `dW = sigma u v^T` with `||u|| = ||v|| = 1`, `v` supported on 64 columns. `K0` is the matrix of the inputs of `mlp.down_proj` (`act_fn(gate_proj(x)) * up_proj(x)`, 3,584-d) of that layer in the **base** model over every position of every **fit** window (the first position included), float32 activations, the second-moment matrix `C = K0 K0^T` accumulated in float64 (streaming: per window, a float32 product on the device, added to a float64 host sum), `N` positions. **Uncentred**, as the proposal's `K0` is. Layer 19's `K0` is taken in the base, not in the model with layer 15 edited: a first-order approximation, stated.

### 5.2 The projection
`C = Q Lambda Q^T` (float64 `eigh`); singular values of `K0` are `s_i = sqrt(N lambda_i)`; **retained** are the `r` directions with `s_i > c * s_max` for the cutoff `c` in **{1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4}** (the proposal's sketch uses `1e-3`; the other two bracket it); `P_c = I - Q_r Q_r^T`; `dW_c = dW P_c`. Everything else in the model is left as in the base. In terms of variance, `c` is `c^2` of the largest eigenvalue: `1e-4`, `1e-6` and `1e-8`. The number of retained directions `r` at each cutoff is reported (**the first thing to look at**: if `r = 3584` at `1e-3`, the proposal's recipe projects onto an empty space and returns zero).

### 5.3 What is reported, and the predictions
* **Survival of the norm**, `S_F(c, layer) = ||dW P_c||_F / ||dW||_F` (`= ||P_c v|| / ||v||`).
* **Survival of the effect**, `S_E(c, layer, domain) = sqrt( E_domain[(v_c . a)^2] / E_domain[(v . a)^2] )`, with `a` the `down_proj` input of an eval token and `v_c = P_c v`: the ratio of the amplitude of the edit's contribution to the residual stream on those tokens (`u` cancels). It is measured on every domain, and **selectivity** is `S_E(math_chat_resp) / S_E(general eval mean)`; a projection that works as the proposal hopes has selectivity above 1 (the edit survives where it is wanted and not elsewhere), but `K0` contains no math, which is exactly what the calibration needs, and a selectivity near 1 says the edit and the general text cannot be separated by this projection.
* **The collateral** of `p1e-2`, `p1e-3`, `p1e-4` (4.2), against `edited`.
* **The neurons.** For the 64 edited neurons of each layer against the other 3,520: the root mean square `rms_j` of `a_j` on the fit split, its percentile among the 3,584; `m_rms` = the median over the 3,584 neurons of `rms_j`; the **firing rate** `f_j` = the share of eval positions with `|a_j| >= 0.1 m_rms`, per domain. **C_fire** holds iff the median over the 64 of `f_j` (all eval domains pooled) is at least **0.25** in both layers (a typical edited neuron is at least a tenth as loud as a typical neuron on a quarter of general text).
* **A control for "generic".** 200 random supports of 64 columns carrying the published `v`'s values in random order (seed 20261106), each projected the same way: the distribution of `S_F` and the percentile of the published `S_F` in it.
* **bf16 survival** (rollback artifact, 1): `||bf16(W_base + dW_c) - W_base||_F / ||dW_c||_F`, with torch's round-to-nearest-even bf16, for the published edit and the three projected ones. For the published edit it is the rounding that the published checkpoint already contains (the value is about 1 by definition: the checkpoint is `bf16(W_base + dW)`); for the projected ones it says whether a bf16 checkpoint of them would hold the edit.

**Predictions, fixed now (they may be wrong; that is the point of writing them):**
* **P1.** If the edited neurons fire on general text (**C_fire**), the projection at the proposal's cutoff removes most of the edit: `S_F(1e-3) <= 0.5` **in both layers** (that is, at least 75% of the squared norm is gone). If **C_fire holds and `S_F(1e-3) > 0.5`**, the inference "fires on general text, therefore the projection removes it" is **falsified** for this edit, and the reading is that `v` sits in directions of low variance although its coordinates are loud (reported with the percentile among the random supports). If **C_fire fails**, the premise of the prediction is false and P1 is not evaluated (reported either way).
* **P2.** `S_E(1e-3)` on the pooled general eval tokens is at most 0.5 in both layers. (If `S_F` and `S_E` disagree by more than a factor 2 the reason is that the projection is large where the tokens are not; reported.)
* **P3.** The cutoff `1e-4` retains all but at most a few percent of the 3,584 directions (`r >= 3,400`), so `S_F(1e-4)` is small (at most 0.2) at both layers: at that cutoff the recipe projects to nearly nothing. (The variance floor at `1e-8` is below float32 rounding of the accumulated covariance, about `1e-7`, so the smallest retained directions are partly noise: stated, reported.)
* **P4.** The selectivity of 5.3 is **below 2** at `1e-3` (the general text and the math text overlap in the 3,584 directions): the projection cannot be made to keep the target effect while removing the collateral. This is the weakest of the four and the one the GPU run would test.
* **P5 (the published edit's neurons are ordinary).** The median percentile of `rms_j` of the 64 edited neurons is between 0.25 and 0.75 in both layers.
**What would change the plan:** if P1 holds (the projection removes most of the edit), E2d (the behavioural sweep) is run for the three cutoffs against the **published edit's** W1 verdict and is expected to show a loss of gain along with the loss of collateral; if P1 is falsified, the `p1e-3` edit is the first candidate for the L4 (it keeps the edit and is expected to lower the collateral).

## 6. E1: find the direction yourself, and choose the neurons (pre-registered; later, after the W1 cloud run lands)

### 6.1 Data
From the W1 cloud run's **base** generations (200 GSM8K problems, thinking on, cap 8,192, sampled: `weight-space.md` 13.3), read once the job is done, copied out of `results/w1_cloud`, never written there. **Looping span**: for a generation that hit the token limit, the tokens in its tail-periodic region (the detector of 4.4, found for the longest period that explains the tail) minus the first cycle; **non-looping**: the first 25% of every generation (its tokens before any repeated region) and every generation that ended with a stop id. Problems 0 to 99 fit, 100 to 199 held out; a rule or a direction is chosen on 0 to 99 and scored on 100 to 199.

### 6.2 The direction `u` (residual space, 1,024-d, per layer 15 and 19)
The contrastive generalised eigenproblem of the proposal: `C_t v = lambda (C_0 + 1e-4 tr(C_0)/d I) v`, `C` the second moments of the **output of the layer's MLP** (the input to the residual add) on looping and on non-looping tokens; `u` the top eigenvector; alternatives printed with it: the difference of means, a logistic probe, the top gradient direction of the loop loss. **Direction found** iff `|cos(u_found, u_published)| >= 0.7` in both layers (`u_published` from the SVD of the diff). It is also reported when the cosine is smaller: a different direction that reaches the same gain is a result, not a failure.

### 6.3 The neuron selection (the gap): rules fixed now, each takes `u` and returns 64 of 3,584 columns of `down_proj`
Notation: `W_j` is column `j` of the base `down_proj` (the neuron's write vector, 1,024-d); `a_j` its activation.
* **R0** random 64 (control).
* **R1 weight-only, direction-aligned:** the 64 largest `|u . W_j|`. Needs no data beyond `u`.
* **R2 weight-only, normalised:** the 64 largest `|u . W_j| / ||W_j||`.
* **R3 activation-difference-weighted:** the 64 largest `|mean_loop(a_j) - mean_non(a_j)| * |u . W_j|`: neurons that both differ between the two kinds of text and write along `u`.
* **R4 gradient-based (first-order attribution):** the 64 largest `E_loop[ |a_j * d ell / d a_j| ]`, with `ell` the log-probability of the token that continues the loop (the repeated token) under the base, `a_j` the input of `down_proj`.
* **R5 neuron-space contrastive:** the generalised eigenproblem of 6.2 solved in the 3,584-d `a` space, the 64 largest `|v_j|` of the top eigenvector.
* **R_pub** the published 64 (oracle reference).
**Value on the support** (same for every rule, so that rules are compared on the selection): `dW[:, j] = -(u . W_j) u` for `j` in the support (the edit that removes direction `u` from the column), scaled by `alpha`; `alpha = 1` is the full removal. (Whether the published edit has this form is measured at once, section 6.4.)
**How a rule is scored.** (i) **Overlap** with the published support (a hypergeometric test: 64 of 3,584 drawn twice overlap in 1.14 columns on average); (ii) the **index correlation**: the Spearman coefficient between a rule's score and the neuron index `j` across the 3,584, to be compared with the same coefficient over the published set's membership (which is strongly negative, 1); (iii) **the proxy of 7.1** at the gate-passing `alpha`; (iv) the behaviour on the L4, for the best rule only (E1's own claim). A rule is **adopted** iff on the held-out problems it reaches at least 50% of the published edit's proxy gain with a passing gate (4.3).

### 6.4 E1a: a weights-only look at the published edit, measured in this PR (cheap, no data)
Before any activation is collected, from `dW` and `W_base` alone: `u`, `v` from the SVD; the regression of `v_j` on `u . W_j` over the 64 columns (slope `c`, Pearson `rho`); the rank of the published columns among the 3,584 by `|u . W_j|` and by `||W_j||`; the Spearman coefficients of the index against each. **Pre-registered:** **H1** `c` in `[-1.2, -0.8]` and `|rho| >= 0.9`: the published edit is "remove `u` from the 64 columns", which is what 6.3's value rule assumes. **H2** at least 48 of the 64 published columns are among the top 96 by `|u . W_j|` (R1 recovers the published support). Chance for H2 is below 1e-30. The index skew of 1 makes me expect **H2 to fail** (**[estimate]**: no weight-only score is known to track the index) and H1 to hold; if H1 holds and H2 fails, the support was chosen with data (R3 to R5 or a mixture), and that is what E1 then has to find.

## 7. The later experiments (scope and cost; none is run here)

### 7.1 E2c: a proxy for the behavioural gain, so that E2's frontier needs no GPU
**[open]** On the W1 base run's looping generations (6.1), the base's probability of continuing the loop: the mean over looping-span positions of `log p(repeated token)` under base and under the variant, and the share of positions where the variant's greedy token leaves the loop. **Validity checks before it is used (all must hold):** the published edit gains more than `rand0`; the proxy's ranking of `e15`, `e19`, `edited` matches the ranking W1's verdict gives (only the full edit is measured by W1; the single layers are an L4 run each: **[estimate]** 2 to 10 hours each); the projected edits' proxy gains fall monotonically with the cutoff's strength. If a check fails, the proxy is not used and the frontier of E2d is the L4's.
**Cost:** a pass over the looping generations (a few hundred thousand tokens), minutes on this Mac; **depends on the W1 cloud run finishing**.

### 7.2 E2d: the behavioural frontier (L4)
`w1.py behave` on a variant checkpoint **in f32 or f16, not bf16** (3, bf16 survival), over the grid {published, `p1e-2`, `p1e-3`, `p1e-4`} x {alpha 1} first (4 L4 runs, each the W1 main run: **[estimate]** hours each by W1's own timing), then alpha in {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25} and rank in {1, 2, 4} for the best. The cheapest first step on the L4 is one run: `p1e-3` against the published edit's W1 verdict (section 11 says which).

### 7.3 E3: signatures across seeds (small transformers; **[estimate]** 1 to 2 days of work, minutes of Mac time per model)
Three to five 2-layer, 4-head transformers, learned absolute positions, no q/k norm, no RoPE, different seeds, trained on the same synthetic task (random tokens, repeated halves) until in-context copying is at least 95% on the repeated half. **Heads are labelled by behaviour** (induction: attention from the second occurrence of a token to the token after its first; previous-token: attention to position -1) with score thresholds 0.5. **Signature** of a head: the 8 largest singular values of `OV_h = W_V^h W_O^h` normalised by their Frobenius norm, the top singular values of `QK_h = W_Q^h (W_K^h)^T` normalised the same way, and, for a head in the second layer, the K-composition score with each first-layer head, taken as the maximum over first-layer heads. **Invariance tests first** (a random `GL(d_head)` applied to a copy of a head leaves the signature unchanged to `1e-5`; permuting the heads in a layer permutes the signature set; a mutant that uses `W_Q` alone fails). **Pass:** with leave-one-seed-out nearest-centroid classification of heads into {induction, previous-token, other}, balanced accuracy at least 0.9 on the held-out seed for every seed, **and** the between-seed distance of the induction signatures is at most half the distance to the other-class centroid. **Fail** (the proposal's D3): either condition false. A failure ends the circuit-IR idea here (section 8).

### 7.4 E4: compile and splice an induction head (W2 with a new target; **[estimate]** 3 to 5 days, CPU/MPS)
The model of 7.3 with the induction mechanism removed (the second layer's induction heads zeroed and the model fine-tuned on the same data without repeats, or a model trained with a context of 1 repeat-free span), **function-preserving growth**: two new heads (a previous-token head in layer 0, a copy head in layer 1) with zero output projections. The writer (`harness.py` with a new `task.py`, scorer = in-context copy accuracy on random-token repeats on a held-out set, plus the original task's loss) writes the new heads' `W_Q W_K W_V W_O` from the specification, with a budget `B` of counted forward passes in {0, 10, 50}, 3 runs each (W2's arms); the gradient arm trains the same two heads (everything else frozen) on `N` examples. **Pass (the proposal's D4):** the compiled model reaches copy accuracy at least 0.9 at `B = 0`, loses at most 0.5% on the original task, and the gradient arm needs at least 1,000 steps to reach the compiled accuracy minus one point. **Cost:** an agent run per writer (about 100k tokens, `weight-space.md` 12.1) and minutes of CPU for the gradient arm.

### 7.5 E5: a topic subspace for style (**[estimate]** 1 day)
Local data has no poetry; the contrast is **literary Spanish prose (`es`) against technical English prose (`en_md`)**, which is a style contrast, not poetry. Contrastive subspace at layers 12, 15, 19 on the `fit` split; a rank-`k` edit (`k` in {1, 2, 4}, `alpha` in a small grid) against inference-time steering with the same direction; **style score** = a logistic regression on hashed character 3-grams trained on the eval split's documents (held-out documents scored by it on 64-token continuations) and **coherence** = the gates of 4.3. Pass: an edit that moves the style score by at least 0.2 (on a scale where the classes sit at 0 and 1) with the gates passing. The poetry corpus of 3.3 replaces the contrast when approved.

## 10. What this document does not claim
* That the corpus is "general": it is what is on this machine (3.1), and the repositories dominate it. A domain-level pass here is a statement about those domains.
* That a KL or perplexity gate bounds behaviour: the proposal's own section 3 says no global guarantee exists, and **neither gate says anything about long generations, which is where the edit acts** (windows are at most 2,048 tokens, the repetition probe is 256).
* Anything about the authors' procedure: only about their artifact.
* That `float32` on MPS is the model's behaviour in bf16 on a GPU: the edit's collateral is measured in the float32 widening of the published bf16 weights, the forward the cancho engine's `f32` path also implements (C5 to C7).

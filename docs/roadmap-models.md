# Training and serving real models with cancho-gpu: the gaps, and a plan

> **Status: a plan (a design written before the code), with measured starting points.** Marks as elsewhere: **[measured]**, **[read]** (from source or documents, not run), **[open]**.
> Written after `resident.md` section 14: the training stack reaches 2 TFLOPS on the matrix unit and trains MLPs faster than torch on MPS. **M1 to M3 are built (`resident.md` sections 15 to 17), M4 is built on a synthetic image set (section 18) and M7.1 to M7.3, the forward and backward passes of Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA and a 300-step fine-tuning run equal to peft's, and M7.4, the same on Qwen2.5-1.5B and full fine-tuning of the 0.5B model, are built (sections 20 to 24; 24.7 to 24.9: resumable across sessions, a 300-step run, the 1.5B full step fits); M7.5 part A, a 4-bit base on the 1.5B (QLoRA), is built (section 27) and so is the 7B (section 28)**; M10 (fine-tuning a Qwen3.8-27B-class model) is a feasibility design with probes and a cost model (section 9; nothing trained); the rest below is still a plan.

## 1. What the machine can do, and what cancho-gpu can do now

The machine is an M4 Max, 64 GB, about 14 TFLOPS in float32 and more in f16 on the matrix unit, 546 GB/s **[read, Apple]**; the matrix-unit product `gemm_mma` runs at 2.2 TFLOPS in our generic tiling **[measured]**.
A training run holds, per parameter, an f32 master (4 B), Adam's two moments (8 B) and an f16 copy (2 B): **14 B**, plus activations. That bounds training on this machine to about 1 B parameters; **100 to 300 M parameters is the comfortable range, and what the plan aims at**.
Serving is bounded by weight bytes over bandwidth: a 4-bit 7 B model reads 3.5 GB a token, about 150 tokens a second at the bandwidth, and `cancho-gpu` reaches 39.9 on a 27 B model today (`benchmark.md`).

What exists **[measured]**: dense and mixed-precision MLP training with Adam, minibatch tables, binary files, 4 session directives (`resident`, `table`, `carry`, `steps`), ReLU, sigmoid, softplus, exp, log, rsqrt, row reductions; decode kernels (RoPE, KV cache, attention in two kernels, gated-delta, NVFP4 matvec) and runners for Qwen2 and Qwen3.8; an OpenAI-compatible server; a BPE tokenizer and sampler.
What does not: everything in section 2.

## 2. The gaps, by what each model needs

| gap | needed by | what it is | how, in the surface | size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **G1 embedding lookup and its gradient** | every LLM, speech | a table row chosen by a token id; the gradient adds into rows | forward: an **index-valued load** (a tile whose row offset is data); backward: **scatter-add**, which needs atomics or a sort-and-segment reduction. For a small vocabulary (characters: 65) a one-hot matrix and `mma`/`mma_tn` do both with no new operation | M (atomics: L) |
| **G2 normalisation, forward and backward** | transformers, CNNs | LayerNorm/RMSNorm/BatchNorm | `rowsum` gives the forward; the backward needs column sums, which a product with a row of ones gives (`rowvec_mm` already is that) | S |
| **G3 softmax and cross-entropy over a vocabulary** | LLMs | row softmax, its backward `p (dp - rowsum(dp p))`, the loss with an integer target | all `rowsum`/`exp`; the target needs G1's index-valued load (or a one-hot for small vocabularies) | S |
| **G4 attention for training** | transformers | causal scores, softmax, `P V` and the backward `dV = P^T dO`, `dP = dO V^T`, `dS = P (dP - rowsum(dP P))`, `dQ = dS K`, `dK = dS^T Q` | every product is an `mma` layout (now available); batched heads are a second grid axis; the causal mask is an additive table. A fused (flash-style) form needs a running max (**`max a b`** in the surface and loops carrying more than one tile, `kernels.md` section 4) | M; flash form L |
| **G5 activations** | all | GELU, SiLU, tanh and their derivatives | element-wise, a few lines each in the lowering and the interpreter | S |
| **G6 optimiser completeness** | all | AdamW decay, global-norm gradient clipping, the schedule | decay is element-wise; the clip is a reduction over every tensor (a kernel summing squares, then a scale in the Adam step); the schedule is already a `table` | S |
| **G7 dropout and random numbers** | regularised training | a deterministic counter-based RNG | a `rand` operation (Philox) in the surface, with a reference in Python | M |
| **G8 range of f16** | deep or large models | f16 overflows at 65504 | bf16 tiles on the matrix unit (does Metal on this OS take `bfloat`? **[open]**), or loss scaling | S to M |
| **G9 data beyond memory** | any real dataset | tables are loaded into the interpreter's binary64 memory (8 bytes an element) before being uploaded; integer ids need an integer dtype | upload tables to the device directly from the file in chunks, `i32` dtype, no interpreter copy | M |
| **G10 checkpoints** | training then serving | a session can only print text | `save P.PARAM FILE` (binary) and a reader in the runners. **Built** (`resident.md` section 19): `save P.OUT FILE [f32\|f16]` and the request `checkpoint`, atomic, with `fs_write` added to the session programs' authority by the owner's approval; the reader is `gpt_session.read_checkpoint` | S |
| **G11 convolution** (built for 3x3 stride 1 conv2d, `resident.md` section 18) | vision, speech front-ends | conv2d/conv1d, forward and both gradients | in NHWC with a zero-padded border, a convolution is a **loop over the kernel taps** of `mma` on shifted row windows of one flattened `[N*H*W, C]` matrix (a rectangular load at a computed row offset), and the gradients are `mma_nn` and `mma_tn` over the same taps: **no new operation**, only the kernels; strides above 1 and max-pooling need `max a b` | M |
| **G12 generic serving** | any open model | a decoder runner for any llama-family model (RMSNorm, RoPE, grouped-query attention, SwiGLU), from its files, with a prefill on the matrix unit | today's runners hardcode Qwen2 and Qwen3.8, and prefill takes 8 tokens at a time; a configuration-driven runner on `mma` | L |
| **G13 weight formats** | serving | bf16/f16 dense (trivial) and 4/8-bit groups (Q4_K_M, Q8_0, AWQ/GPTQ) | each is a dequantising matvec like `matvec_fp4`; NVFP4 exists | S per format |
| **G14 contexts beyond 1024** | long prompts | split-KV attention with its merge | G4's running max and multi-tile loops | L |
| **G15 audio** | speech | log-mel spectrogram | a short-time Fourier transform is a product with a precomputed cosine/sine matrix (no FFT needed at n_fft = 400), then the mel filter matrix: two `mma`; reading a wav is host code | S |

## 3. The milestones, each ending at a number

Each milestone is a separate change, designed before it is built, with gates that can fail, and its result is **compared with torch on this machine the way `resident.md` section 13 and 14 compare MLPs**: the same data, the same start, the same batches, the loss curves compared, then the time.

**M1. A character-level GPT trains. Built (`resident.md` section 15): the gradients of all 28 tensors within 8e-4 of a float64 backward, a 10.8 M-parameter GPT at 1.376 nats a character, 37 ms a step against torch MPS's 41.** (original plan follows) (G2 to G6, G10; no G1: 65 characters are a one-hot). A 6-layer, 384-wide, 6-head GPT on the Shakespeare characters (about 10 M parameters), context 256, as in nanoGPT **[read]**.
Exit: the loss after 100 steps equals a float64 numpy implementation's to 1e-3 (CPU session) and torch's to 3 digits; the validation loss falls under 1.6 nats; step time and total time are within 1.5 of torch on MPS, or better.
New kernels: LayerNorm forward/backward, causal attention forward/backward (non-fused first), GELU, the cross-entropy, AdamW. New language: `gelu`, `tanh` (G5), `max a b`.

**M2. Serve what M1 trained. Built (`resident.md` section 16): all 576 greedy tokens equal torch's, 650 tokens a second against torch MPS's 340 (recompute) and 273 (cached); the checkpoint is host-side, no key/value cache yet.** (original plan follows) (G10, G12 in its smallest form). `save`/load the checkpoint; a decoder runner for this GPT with a KV cache (the attention-decode kernels exist); sample text with the existing sampler.
Exit: the generated tokens equal torch's greedy tokens for the same weights; tokens a second against torch on MPS.

**M3. A real vocabulary and a real dataset. Built except for what is named (`resident.md` sections 17 and 19): a 2,048-token BPE, a 27 M-parameter GPT (81 ms a step against torch's 86) and, through the device-side `save` of section 18, a 59.95 M-parameter one (8 layers, d 768): 167.8 ms a step against torch MPS's 196.7 (15% faster) and 201.1 with mixed precision, validation loss 3.106 against 3.114 and 3.278, the curves within 0.013 nats at every point; no gather/scatter needed at that vocabulary. Not done: TinyStories (it needs a download), vocabularies above about 2,048, memory against torch, contexts above 128.** (original plan follows) (G1, G7, G9). Index-valued load and scatter-add (atomics, or the sort-and-segment form measured against them), device-direct tables, a BPE-tokenised corpus; a 30 to 60 M parameter GPT on TinyStories-scale text.
Exit: the loss curve agrees with torch's; tokens a second in training against torch; memory against torch.

**M4. A CIFAR-10 convolutional network. Partly built (`resident.md` section 18): a 0.85 M-parameter ResNet (13 convolutions, BatchNorm, residual blocks) trained in a Metal session with no new operation, every kernel and the network held to a float64 specification; on a synthetic image set (CIFAR-10 is not on this machine and its download needs the owner's permission) the curves equal torch's (final test accuracy 0.911 against 0.913 and 0.908) and a step takes 17.6 ms against torch MPS's 10.6; not done: CIFAR-10 itself and its 90%, max-pooling (average pooling was used, so `max a b` was not needed), augmentation.** (original plan follows) (G11, BatchNorm of G2, `max`). A ResNet-style network of about 1 M parameters. Exit: 90% test accuracy in the time torch needs on this machine, the curves agreeing.

**M5. Serve an open LLM** (G12, G13, G14 optional). A llama-family model of 1 to 3 B parameters (Llama-3.2, Qwen2.5) from its safetensors or GGUF files with a mixed-precision prefill. Exit: its tokens equal the reference's on a fixed prompt set (`scripts/model-check.sh` style) and its speed is reported against Ollama and MLX-LM as `benchmark.md` does.

**M6. Speech** (G11 for conv1d, G15). Whisper-tiny/base inference: log-mel, encoder, decoder with cross-attention. Exit: the transcript of a fixed clip equals the reference's, and the real-time factor against torch on MPS is reported; fine-tuning only after that.

## 4. What this plan does not do

* **Multi-GPU or distributed training**, and models beyond what 64 GB trains (1 B parameters at the very most).
* **CUDA**: every new operation is lowered for both targets and held by goldens, but no kernel here has run on NVIDIA. The numbers above are Apple's; an NVIDIA machine would measure the same milestones separately (the operand-layout text of `mma_nn`/`mma_tn` for CUDA is generated, not run).
* **A model zoo or a Python front end.** Models are written as sessions of `.chx` kernels (generated by scripts, as `mlp_session.py` is); a higher-level builder is a later question and is not needed to reach any milestone.

## 5. Order, and the first step

G5 and `max a b` (S, language), then G2 and G4 forward and backward as kernels held to numpy, then the GPT session (M1). M1 needs no new directive and no new dtype, so it can start at once; it is the first test of whether the kernel set reaches a transformer.

## 6. M7: fine-tune a Qwen with LoRA (design, written before the code)

> Marks as above. Everything here is a plan except the torch baseline, which is measured (`examples/qwen/torch_baseline.py`).

**The target.** Qwen2.5-0.5B and 1.5B are on this machine already (the Hugging Face cache; bf16 safetensors; no download needed), and torch 2.12, transformers 5.2 and peft 0.21 are installed, so the reference is the real library. Both are Qwen2 models **[measured, `config.json`]**: 0.5B has 24 layers, hidden 896, 14 query heads over 2 key/value heads of 64, SwiGLU of 4,864, vocabulary 151,936 and tied input/output embeddings (136 M of its 494 M parameters); 1.5B has 28 layers, hidden 1,536, 12 query heads over 2, SwiGLU of 8,960, tied embeddings (233 M of 1.54 B).
**The baseline to beat** (torch MPS, rank-16 LoRA on every linear layer of the blocks, 4,096 tokens a step as 8 sequences of 512, AdamW, random ids, speed only **[measured]**):

| model | dtype | trainable | ms a step | tokens a second | peak driver memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen2.5-0.5B | float32 | 8.8 M | 1,230 | 3,330 | 27.2 GB |
| Qwen2.5-0.5B | float16 autocast | 8.8 M | 1,157 | 3,540 | 26.0 GB |
| Qwen2.5-1.5B | float16 autocast, 4 x 512 | 18.5 M | 1,388 | 1,476 | 20.5 GB |

**What to expect, and what not.** A LoRA step costs about `4 N tokens` FLOPs (forward and the input gradient of every frozen weight; the frozen weights need no weight gradient), which for the 0.5B model and 4,096 tokens is 8.1 TFLOP; torch's 1.157 s is **7.0 TFLOPS**, about what our own GPT runs reach (7.2 to 8.8 TFLOPS in `resident.md` sections 15 and 19). So unlike the small models, where cancho-gpu won by 6 to 15% on launch overhead and fusion, **a 0.5B model is matrix-product bound and parity is the realistic speed exit**. What cancho-gpu can win is memory: the weights as f16 (1 GB), adapters with Adam (0.12 GB) and about 10 GB of activations and logits, derived from the saved tensors (about 55 KB a token a layer, 2.5 GB of f32 logits and 1.2 GB of their gradient) against torch's 26 GB. **[derived, to be measured]**

**What is not needed.**
* **No embedding gather or scatter-add (G1).** With LoRA the embeddings (tied with the output layer) are frozen: the token embeddings of a step are rows of a fixed table, so the host writes them as an f16 `table` (4,096 x 896 x 2 B = 7.3 MB a step) and the output layer's backward is an ordinary product with the frozen matrix. Full fine-tuning (training the embeddings) is the later M7.4 and is when G1 is paid.
* **No flash attention up to a context of 1,024.** The scores of a head are tiled over key columns and written to memory, the softmax takes a row of 512 or 1,024 columns a threadgroup (`ce` already runs a 2,048-column row with `bm=1`), `P V` and the backward products are the ones of section 15. Probabilities cost 14 heads x t^2 x 2 B a sequence a layer: 7 MB at 512, 29 MB at 1,024, 4 sequences x 24 layers x 29 MB = 2.8 GB. Beyond 1,024 it is G14.
* **No `max a b`.** The softmax and the large-vocabulary loss are three kernels: per-chunk maxima and sums of exponentials, a combine over the chunk row, and the normalisation / gradient pass, all `rowmax`, `rowsum`, `exp`.

**What is needed** (each a kernel held to a numpy specification, as section 15 does):
1. **RMSNorm forward and backward** (the `ported/rmsnorm.chx` is batch 1): `rowsum` of squares, `rsqrt`; the gain's gradient is a column sum (`rowvec_mm`).
2. **RoPE forward and backward** on a `[tokens, heads x 64]` matrix with the position of each row: the backward is the inverse rotation. The position of a tile is affine in the grid coordinates if the grid is (sequence, row tile), so no division of an index is needed.
3. **Grouped-query attention**: 14 query heads read 2 key/value heads, and a head's group is `h / 7`, a division the surface forbids. Grid over (sequence, key/value head, row tile) with a `for` over the 7 queries of the group; the key/value gradients sum over that loop. **[open]**: measure this against repeating K and V to 14 heads with a copy kernel and summing back with a small product.
4. **SwiGLU forward and backward** (`silu` is `x sigmoid(x)`, both exist) and the **biases of q, k and v** (`bias_h` exists).
5. **The loss over 151,936 columns**: the three kernels above; the logits of a step are 2.5 GB in f32, which fits, and the head product `[4096, 896] x [896, 151936]` is 0.56 TFLOP forward, a quarter of the step: its tile schedule gets its own measurement.
6. **LoRA**: `y = x W^T + (alpha / r) (x A^T) B^T`. Both added products are the existing `mma` layouts with a 16-wide middle dimension; `B` starts at zero; gradients `dB = s dy^T (x A^T)`, `dA = s (dy B)^T x`, and `dx` gets two more terms. Adam on f32 masters, f16 copies, as section 14.
7. **Weights**: a converter script writes the safetensors as f16 and f32 binary files in the order the session names them (bf16 narrows to f16 exactly for every value in its range, `inference.md` section 1, which the existing runner already relies on; a check for values outside it is part of the converter).
8. **Data**: Qwen's own tokenizer (`tokenizers`) on the Markdown, Rust and cancho files of this machine (the `bpe.py` corpus), packed to sequences of 512; held-out loss as the measure. No download.

## 7. M8: does fine-tuning a small model on cancho make it better at cancho-code's job? (design, written before the results)

> Marks as above. This section is written **before any tuned model exists**; the decision rule below is fixed now and is not edited after a tuned result is seen. Facts that turn out different are corrected in place, with the date.

**The question.** cancho-code (`../cancho-code`) writes cancho programs unit by unit: a plan of small units (a signature, a description, examples), a model asked for each unit, the cancho compiler and the unit's examples as the judge, a compile-fix loop of up to 6 attempts, and a hidden oracle that grades the finished library. Does a small local model **fine-tuned on cancho sources** do that job better than **the same model untuned**?

**The model, and one serving path.** Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (in the Hugging Face cache; **no download of any kind**). It is served to cancho-code for every arm by one process, `examples/qwen/serve_ollama.py`: an Ollama-API endpoint (`POST /api/generate`, the one cancho-code's `--provider ollama` agent speaks) over transformers on the Metal GPU, f16 weights, the Qwen2.5 chat template around the prompt as one user turn, sampling with the request's temperature (cancho-code's ollama agent sends 0.2, `src/ollama.cho`), top-k 20 and top-p 0.8 (the model's `generation_config.json`), no repetition penalty, a seed per request (`--seed` x 1,000,003 + request number, logged). A tuned arm differs from an untuned one in **the weights (LoRA r=16 merged into the f16 weights at load) and the prompt, nothing else**. The decode settings are in the shim, identical for all arms.
*Why a Python shim and not cancho:* the native server (`cancho-gpu-serve`, `serving.md`) serves the 27B only, its cache is 1,024 positions, it has no prefix cache and it refuses a prompt that does not fit; the primer of the arms below is **10,666 Qwen tokens [measured, the `cancho agent-guidelines` of the local compiler, Qwen's tokenizer]**. The 1.5B session path (`qwen_session.py`) runs 512-token windows and recomputes the window for every new token. There is no cancho-native path that serves this model at this context, so the shim is Python (about 250 lines; `docs/serving.md` is the native one for the 27B). **[read]**

**The benchmark (fixed, hidden, never trained on).** The 13 hand-planned library items of `cancho-code/eval/`: basics, tokenbucket, roman, base64, duration, editdistance, sortedmap, jsonptr, expr, glob, semver, ini, csv. Each is built by `eval/run_item.py <item> --provider ollama ...` from its hand-written plan (`eval/<item>/plan.json`: the model writes units only) and graded by the item's hidden oracle (`eval/audit_<item>.py`; the model never sees it). Run settings, the same for every arm, are cancho-code's defaults: complete-file replies (not `--patch`), at most 6 attempts a unit, a same-signature stop, one unit at a time (`--jobs 1`), 300 s a model call, `--num-ctx 16384`, `--max-tokens` 3072, temperature 0.2. **N = 5 runs per item per arm** (65 runs an arm).
*Reference points:* Claude Haiku 5.5, published in `cancho-code/eval/results/2026-10-14-haiku-5-5.{txt,json}` (13 items x 3 runs, 38 of 39 builds finished, 7,505 of 7,581 oracle points; Claude is not re-run and no API key is used).

**The arms.**
* **A** base Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct + the standard primer (`--primer`: the output of `cancho agent-guidelines`, the file cancho-code's README names) in context.
* **B** base, no primer.
* **C** tuned + primer. **D** tuned, no primer. (Phase 2.)
* **E** (optional, only if time) tuned with retrieval of the nearest examples.
"Internalised the language" means the model with no primer is as good as the untuned model with it.

**Metrics** (per item and overall; `eval/m8_report.py` of cancho-code computes them from each run directory).
1. **Oracle pass fraction**: the hidden oracle's passed/total for the finished `project.cho` of a run (0 when the run did not build; this is how `eval/results` counts). Mean over the 65 runs of an arm, every run weighted the same.
2. **Units verified** (of the plan's units), **attempts used** (model calls that produced a program or were refused, per run), the project verdict.
3. **First-attempt compile rate**: of the first attempt of each unit that was tried, the share the compiler accepted (a refusal, a reply with no program, and a timeout are all not accepted). **Rate after the compile-fix loop**: of the units tried, the share that ended `verified` (the compiler and the unit's examples). Refusal rule tags are tallied from the journal.
4. **Unit-level pass@1** = the share of tried units verified by attempt 0 (accepted by the compiler and by the unit's own examples); **"verified within 4 attempts"** is reported in place of pass@4: the loop feeds the compiler's refusal back, so its attempts are **not independent samples** and the number is not the unbiased pass@4 estimator. The harness cannot draw independent samples of one unit; labelled so.
5. Tokens a second and per-request latency of the shim (the served runs' own log).

**Pre-registered decision rule** (fixed before any tuned result exists):
* *Statistic*: S(arm) = the mean oracle pass fraction over the arm's 65 (item, run) observations. *Uncertainty*: the 95% bootstrap confidence interval of S(arm), 10,000 resamples of the 65 observations with replacement (observations are resampled, items and runs together, which counts the spread between items), numpy seed 20261103; **h(arm)** is half its width.
* **C beats A** iff S(C) − S(A) > h(A). **D beats B** iff S(D) − S(B) > h(B). **D has internalised the language** iff S(D) ≥ S(A). If the difference is within h the answer is "no detectable effect", not "no effect".
* *Floor*: if S(A) < 0.02 (or S(B) < 0.02) the primary comparison for that pair is **declared floor-limited**: it is still computed and reported, but the reading rests on the secondary measures: first-attempt compile rate and the rule-tag distribution of refusals, with the same bootstrap half-width test applied to the compile rate (resampling the 65 runs' per-run rates). A tuned arm that rises above a floored untuned arm is reported as an increase from the floor, with h, and not as "better at the job" unless it also finishes builds.
* The result is reported **whichever way it falls**, with failure examples (the refusal and the model's reply) from the journals.

**Note, 2026-11-04 (written before any tuned result exists; the rule above is unchanged).** The first run of arms A and B (section 7.2, now labelled pre-fix) exposed two defects of the harness: the refusal text quoted a line of `project.cho` under the line number of another file (`main.cho`), and a reply with code but no `edition N;` line was reported as the generic `no-program`. Both were fixed in cancho-code (the strict gate that decides what is a verified program was not relaxed; code with no `edition N;` line now gets its own refusal, exit 13, which tells the model what is missing). Because the fixes change what the model is told, **A and B were re-run from scratch** under the fixed harness, same items, runs and settings, before any tuned model was trained; the pre-fix results are kept. C and D run under the fixed harness only.

**What is not claimed.** One model size, one seed of training, one machine, one benchmark whose items are small libraries; the temperature is the agent's (0.2) and the runs sample, so the five runs of an item differ; the serving is f16 transformers, not the quantised build a user would run under Ollama. A rise on these 13 items says nothing about other tasks.

### 8.1 Which vision job

The candidate jobs below are therefore **the maintainer's recommendation and my own guesses, not requirements of the repos**.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### 8.2 The ops a ResNet-18-class backbone needs, and what the surface can say (probes: `examples/cnn/probes/`, `tests/probes.py`, in the gate)

| Need | Result [measured] |
|---|---|
| strided window (`load x[...; ...; 2]`) | refused, `syntax`: a window has offsets and extents, no step |
| a gather at an offset that is a product of two coordinates | refused, `offset` |
| unary minus in an offset (`-wp`) | refused, `syntax`; written `c - wp` |
| `abs`, `max a b` | no such operation: `syntax` |
| **elementwise maximum** | **expressible**: `s = step(a - b)`, `m = s a - (s - 1) b`; `s` is exactly 0 or 1 and one product is exactly 0, so the result is **bit for bit `np.maximum`** and `s` is the max-pool's mask (probe `max_select`, run on the interpreter; a literal on the *left* of a tile, `1 - s`, is refused `shape`) |
| a running maximum *and its index* in one loop | refused, `carry` (a loop carries one tile): the nine candidates of a 3x3 window are unrolled |
| 7x7 stem as 49 taps | accepted (taps are loops, the shift is affine); but with stride 1 only |
| **stride 2** | not in the window syntax; **a permutation instead**: space to depth is a product with 0/1 selection matrices, after which a stride-2 3x3 convolution is a stride-1 2x2 convolution (taps -1, 0) of 4c channels, a 1x1 stride-2 is the (0,0) quarter of those channels, and the 7x7 stride-2 stem is a 4x4 stride-1 window with taps -2 .. +1 (`resident.md` 26.1) |
| a tap that reaches 2 pixels past an edge | works with the existing 1-pixel border: a flat shifted read past the edge lands on the neighbouring row's border cell (checked against an explicit zero-padded convolution, `tests/resnet.py`); reach 3 would not |
| BatchNorm statistics in the convolution's epilogue | refused, `type`: the accumulator is a fragment (accumulate, store, carry; no `rowsum`, no `* 2.0`). **This is what fixes the launch count** (8.4) |
| a grid whose size does not divide | refused, `offset` (never silently dropped rows) |
| NHWC / channels-last; 3x3 pad 1; residual add; global average; BatchNorm forward and backward (also without the ReLU for the shortcut's) | built, on the M4 CNN's padded-matrix layout; **NCHW was not tried** |
| tile budget at robot size | two refusals found only at 96x128 (`budget`): the nine staged candidates of max-pool at 64 channels, 16 rows (43,008 B > 32,768) and the global average at 512 channels, 15 rows (33,792 B); the tile row counts are chosen to fit |

**Max-pool is built without a new operation** (`maxpool_fwd`/`maxpool_bwd`), with torch's tie rule (first maximum in row-major order), checked against the image-level max-pool on inputs with ties.

### 8.3 Synthetic data for the pose estimator (a plan; nothing here is built)

* **Renderer [choice]**: a numpy renderer written for this, from the chain of `cancho-robot/tests/fixtures/urdf/so101_new_calib.urdf` (the same joint origins and limits the controller's `so101.cho` was generated from; the URDF's STL meshes are *not* in the repository, so the links are drawn as thick capsules between the frames, a gripper jaw as a second capsule that opens with the gripper angle). A z-buffer of capsules, a camera with pose and field of view randomised around a nominal mounting, a randomised background (gradients, clutter blobs), light direction and contrast, noise, blur, and an 8-bit grey output at 96x128. The labels are the angles used; the FK gives the gripper position as a second target.
* **Split [choice, fixed before any training]**: poses are sampled from the joint limits of the URDF; the held-out set is a *band of the pose space*, not random frames: every pose with `shoulder_pan` in a held-out interval, or `wrist_flex` in its extreme third, plus an interior set of held-out poses with the same ranges as training. Reported separately: the interior error and the band error. Camera and background parameters of the test set are drawn from fresh seeds.
* **What a sim-to-real caveat means here.** The renderer has no meshes, no real lighting model, no lens distortion, no motion blur, no occlusion by cables and the table. A held-out error on its frames says that *the training stack learns a pose regression to the number torch reaches on the same data*; it says **nothing about the error on the real arm's frames**, and no conclusion in this document or its numbers should be read as one. The plan to close the gap is real: fine-tune on a few real frames paired with the joint angles the controller reads (no demonstrations are needed, only a recording of the arm moving), and report the real-frame error as its own number.
* **Real data needed [request, not satisfied]**: a few hundred frames of the real arm with its joint angles, a camera mounting (extrinsics, field of view) and which camera sees the arm; none is on this machine, and no camera image may be committed to any repository (`cancho-robot/docs/design.md`), so the set is derived data or stays off-repo.

### 8.4 Launches per step (the M4 CNN's 1.6x gap)

**Measured by counting the programs of one step's session** (`bench_rn.py launches`, no GPU): the M4 CNN has 186 (reproducing `resident.md` 18.2) and the ResNet-18-class network **287**: the optimiser 61 (one program per tensor: 40 for the BatchNorm vectors, 21 for the weights), the convolutions 79 (20 forward, 19 input gradient, 20 weight gradient, 20 sums of its pieces), **BatchNorm 120** (six programs per layer: `bn_part`, `bn_fin`, `bn_apply`, `bn_bwd_part`, `bn_bwd_fin`, `bn_bwd_apply`), the rest 27 (space to depth, max-pool, residual adds, head). GPU time by kernel kind was not measured for the ResNet (8.5).
**Fusions the surface allows [proposals, none built]**: (1) *finish BatchNorm in its consumers*: `bn_fin` and `bn_bwd_fin` are tiny reductions over at most a few hundred rows; the apply kernels could reduce the partial sums themselves and drop 40 launches (14%), at the price of every apply instance repeating the reduction. (2) *One optimiser program for several tensors*: the surface has no way to give one kernel a list of differently-shaped tensors, but the 40 BatchNorm vectors have only the layer's channel count; a flat `[1, sum c]` parameter vector for all BatchNorm scales, with each backward writing its gradient into a window of one buffer, is not expressible (outputs are separate buffers, and `alias` is reuse of a whole buffer), so this needs a session change (a window directive), not a kernel change: it would remove up to 38 launches. (3) *Fusing the apply kernels with the next convolution's input* is refused by the same `type` rule that forbids the epilogue statistics. Without a change to the language or the session, **the reachable reduction is (1), about 14%**, an **[estimate]** of launches, not of time.

### 8.5 Milestones, each ending at a number (exit criteria stated before building)

* **V1: the backbone's forward and backward equal torch's** (*built: `resident.md` 26*). Exit: (a) the numpy specification equals torch float64 autograd on the 11.18 M-parameter network at 96x128 to 1e-9; (b) the session's loss within 2e-3 relative of torch float32's and the gradients of all tensors with cosine at least 0.98, on the CPU interpreter and on Metal; (c) each new kernel against numpy, with mutants; (d) the surface's refusals recorded with their tags. **Met** except as stated in 26.4 (the full-size network does not fit the CPU interpreter's memory, so (b) is at reduced width on the CPU and full width on Metal once; GPU timing is a TODO).
* **V2: a training step equal to torch's, with speed and memory against torch on MPS.** Exit: the loss curve of 200 AdamW steps within the spread of two torch seeds; **ms a step at batch 32 and 96x128 within 1.25x of torch MPS float32**, and peak memory not above torch's; launches per step at most 250. (The M4 CNN was 1.6x.)
* **V3: the pose estimator trained on synthetic renders, with a held-out metric against a torch run.** Exit: on the held-out *band* of 8.3, the mean absolute joint error of the cancho-gpu run within 10% of a torch run's from the same start, data and schedule, and the interior error reported; the absolute thresholds are fixed when the baseline torch run exists and before the cancho run is made. A sim number only.
* **V4: the inference path.** Exit: the half-width model exported to int8 and run on the Pi 5 at the measured frames a second (the **estimate** above is the claim), its error against the float model at most 1 degree worse on the synthetic test set, and a one-page note of what the `Camera` capability's greyscale frame and the FPGA's deterministic path must provide (size, rate, timestamp). The model's output reaches the safety core only as an advisory cross-check.

## 9. M10: fine-tuning a Qwen3.8-27B-class model (a feasibility design; measured where marked)

> **Status: a design with measurements, nothing trained.** Marks as above: **[measured]** (a command is named), **[read]** (source or documents, not run), **[estimate]** (arithmetic from measured numbers), **[open]**, and **[read: unverified]** for what is known only from outside this machine.

### 9.1 The model **[read: `config.json` of `mlx-community/Qwen3.8-27B-4bit`, the only local copy; the original `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` cache entry is empty]**
64 layers, every fourth (16) full attention, the other 48 `linear_attention` (gated delta net); hidden 5,120, SwiGLU 17,408, vocabulary 248,320 untied, a multi-token-prediction head and a vision tower (both ignored: the loss is the main head's). Full attention: 24 query heads and 4 key/value heads of 256, `q_proj` carries the sigmoid output gate (output 24 x [q 256, gate 256]), per-head RMSNorm of q and k (weight stored as a delta from 1), rotary on 64 of the 256 dimensions (theta 10^7; for text the interleaved m-RoPE is plain RoPE). Gated delta: `in_proj_qkv` (10,240 = 2 x 16 x 128 + 48 x 128), `in_proj_z` (6,144), `in_proj_b` and `in_proj_a` (48 each), a four-tap depthwise convolution with silu over the 10,240 channels, l2-normalised q and k (16 key heads, repeated 3 times to the 48 value heads), `beta = sigmoid(b)`, `g = -exp(A_log) softplus(a + dt_bias)`, the rule on a 128 x 128 f32 state a head, a per-head RMSNorm of the output times `silu(z)`, `out_proj`. The installed transformers 5.2.0 has the architecture (`models/qwen3_5/modeling_qwen3_5.py`), including `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` and the recurrent form. Everything used below is read from that source and from the config; the checkpoint was written by 5.8.0.dev0 and no difference showed in the comparisons.
**Weights on this machine** **[measured: Ollama's manifest for `qwen3.8:27b-mlx`, 18.17 GB]**: NVFP4 (E2M1 codes, an E4M3 scale per 16 values, one f32 per tensor: exactly the format `dequant_fp4` reads, so the shipped bytes are the base as they are; the 7B experiment had to run a host quantiser for 191 s and write 5.5 GB). Per tensor, 0.5625 B a weight plus a 450 B header (`tests/gdn.py` holds this to the manifest: worst 2.6e-5). The language model's blocks are 13.74 GB (MLP 9.63, gated-delta projections 3.17, attention 0.94), the head 0.715 GB (4-bit), the embedding 2.54 GB (bf16, used on the host to look up the rows of a step), the MTP head 0.4 GB and the vision tower 0.9 GB are not needed.

### 9.2 The training form of the gated delta rule (numpy, `examples/qwen35/gdn_ref.py`)
**The rule and its chunked form** **[measured: `tests/gdn.py`, `hf_check.py`]**. Per head (q scaled by 1/sqrt(128) after the l2 norm), the definition is `S = S g; S += (v - S^T k) beta k^T; o = S^T q`. HF's chunked form, chunk C = 64, with `gc` the cumulative log-decay in a chunk, `D[i,j] = exp(gc_i - gc_j)` for `i >= j`, `Ls = tril_strict((beta k) k^T * D)`:
`M = (I + Ls)^-1; u = M (beta v); w = M (beta k e^gc)` and then per chunk, with `S` the state at its start: `v_new = u - w S; o = (q e^gc) S + tril((q k^T) * D) v_new; S = S e^{gc_last} + (k e^{gc_last - gc})^T v_new`.
`chunk_fwd` equals the recurrence to 5.6e-16 (C = 64, 16 and 7, T = 150 so that the last chunk is padded) and HF's `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` patched to float64 to 3.6e-16 (that function rounds to float32 internally; `hf64.py` recompiles the installed source with its float32 casts turned into float64, so the reference is HF's own code).
**The backward** (`chunk_bwd`, derived by hand, reverse over chunks carrying `dS`): `dv_new = Aqk^T do + Kd dS`; `dS_in = dS e^{gc_last} + (q e^gc)^T do - w^T dv_new`; `dw = -dv_new S^T`; `du = dv_new`; `dKd = v_new dS^T` and the gate terms through `e^gc`, `e^{gc_last}`, `e^{gc_last - gc}` and `D`; then `dM = du (beta v)^T + dw (beta k e^gc)^T`, `dLs = tril_strict(-M^T dM M^T)`, the gradients of `beta k`, `k`, `beta`, `gc` through `Ls = (beta k)k^T * D`, and `dg` the reverse cumulative sum of `dgc`. `layer_fwd` / `layer_bwd` add the projections, the conv (taps and silu), the l2 norm, the gates, the repeat (sum over the 3 value heads of a key head), the gated norm and `out_proj`. **All of it against torch autograd through HF's `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet` in binary64** (`hf_check.py`, 18 comparisons, worst relative error **3.4e-14**): the rule (forward and the gradients of q, k, v, g, beta), the layer (forward, dx, all nine parameter gradients, T = 150 = 3 chunks), and **the layer inside a tiny random-init `Qwen3_5ForCausalLM`** (4 layers: 3 linear, 1 full attention, vocabulary 64) with its input and output gradient captured by hooks during a real `loss.backward()` (worst 1.9e-14). Against finite differences without torch: 2.7e-9 (rule) and 2.5e-9 (layer). Mutants (no decay, the solve transposed, `dLs` unmasked, the sign of the column term of the gate gradient) are caught with errors of 1e-1 to 1e1 against the specification's 1e-9 (`tests/gdn.py`).
**What the recurrence needs from the surface** (this is what 9.3 probes):
1. products of 64 x 128 operands (`(beta k) k^T`, `q k^T`, `M x`, `w S`, `(q e^gc) S`, `Aqk v_new`, `Kd^T v_new`) on the matrix unit, each result **feeding elementwise work and then another product**;
2. the decay mask: a column minus a row (`gc_i - gc_j`), an exponential of its lower triangle only, a 0/1 mask;
3. `M = (I + Ls)^-1` for a strictly lower 64 x 64 matrix;
4. the cumulative sum of `g` in a chunk (and its reverse in the backward);
5. the cross-chunk carry of a 128 x 128 f32 state (64 KiB) through 8 sequential chunks at T = 512;
6. the four-tap causal convolution, the repeat of key heads over value heads, l2 norm and gated-norm forward and backward (rowwise, the class of `rms_bwd.chx`).
**A finding that corrects the plan**: the solve cannot be done by repeated squaring, `(I - N)(I + N^2)(I + N^4)...`, which would be six matrix products on the matrix unit and was the first thing to try. The entries of `M` are bounded (at most 1), but the powers of `Ls` grow binomially when keys are correlated: for 64 keys with a shared component (key cosine about 0.5) and beta 0.9 they reach 3e6 (2.4e8 in another draw), and **the squaring solve is off by 8.8e1 in float32** (it loses 7 digits even in float64: 1.2e-7) **while forward substitution is off by 2.7e-7 in float32**; with f16 operands the whole output is off by 1.2 (relative) at key cosine 0.5 and NaN at 0.8. Trained keys are correlated. (`tests/gdn.py` holds the float32 pair and the f16 forward.) **Forward substitution** (HF's loop of 63 dependent rows) is the stable form, and it is sequential.
**Mixed precision** **[measured: numpy emulation of the launches below, f16 operands and f32 accumulation, `chunk_fwd_mp`]**: the relative rms error of the output against binary64 is 4.8e-4 (independent keys), 5.6e-4, 7.7e-4 and 1.1e-3 (key correlation 4) at T = 512, 4 heads of 128 x 128; the existing f16 pipeline's per-layer error is 1e-3 (`resident.md` 20). Synthetic keys and gates, not a trained model: **[open]** on real activations.

**Reading.** A step of the state scan per chunk is three launches, `a`, `bo`, `bu`, **0.571 ms for 384 blocks, 4.6 ms a layer at 4 x 512** (eight chunks), against 8.6 ms for the one-kernel scan on the generic `matmul`: the matrix unit is 1.9x faster even with the launches. The forward of a gated-delta layer's core is then: `kkt` 0.30 + decay 0.11 + solve 5.69 + `u`, `w` (two products, about 0.6) + `Aqk` 0.41 + scan 4.6 + launch gaps and the elementwise conv / gates / norms (about 2.3, **[estimate]**) = **about 14 ms a layer at 4 x 512, 0.67 s for 48 layers** (against about 145 ms of matrix products a layer). **The solve is the largest single item and the one place a compiler feature would pay: a triangular solve, or a fragment that can be read back.**
**The exact gaps** (what is not expressible, and what each costs; the compiler repository is not touched and nothing is filed):
1. **A product cannot feed a product inside a kernel** (a fragment is not scalable, stageable or readable: `type`). Every pair of dependent products is a launch boundary through memory: 3 launches a scan step (24 a layer forward, about 40 backward at T = 512), where a fused kernel would be 1. Cost: the launch floor (about 9 us each) and the round trips of `[64,64]` blocks; small (4.6 ms a layer is mostly arithmetic).
2. **No triangular solve and no loop with a row write**: forward substitution works only as a register loop over a one-hot row (5.7 ms with a [64,64] carried tile; 60 ms with [128,64]). A block-wise inverse (16 x 16 diagonal blocks by forward substitution, then three block products) is not probed **[open]** and would cut it.
3. **No `tril`, `min`/`max`, cumulative sum**: tables for masks, `x - relu(x)` for min, a ones-matrix product for the prefix sum.
4. **No division of a grid coordinate**: grid over key heads, loop of 3.
5. **The 128 x 128 state cannot be one threadgroup's operand** (32,768 B f16 is Metal's whole budget); the value columns are independent, so it is cut in two slices of 64 (`dv/2`): two blocks a head.
6. **`check` does not count the staging scratch of a computed tile** (the `stage_scratch` row): a kernel can pass `check` and be refused at session start.
7. **A generic `matmul` chain** does run (accepted, equal to the specification) but at 1.3 TFLOPS (8.6 ms a layer for the scan): it is the fallback for a first version.
Not probed, and said so: the backward's launches are the forward's with `mma_nn`/`mma_tn` layouts and a reversed loop (same operations: no new refusal is expected, **[open]**); the l2-norm, gated-norm and conv backwards are rowwise or tap-wise elementwise of the class of `rms_bwd.chx` **[read]**.
**The rest of the model, at the real shapes** **[measured: `cancho-gpu check`, both targets]**: `dq_fp4` for every projection (n = 10,240 / 6,144 / 96 / 12,288 / 1,024 / 5,120 / 17,408 / 34,816 / 248,320; k = 5,120 / 6,144 / 17,408; `bn 32 bk 256`), `att_s`, `att_o`, `att_dkv`, `att_sm`, `att_dsoft`, `heads_h`, `unheads` at 24 heads of 256, `swiglu_h2` at 17,408, `rms_h` / `rms_bwd` at 5,120 (**`rms_h` at `bm 4` is refused `budget`, 40,960 B; `bm 2` fits**: the rows a threadgroup takes shrink with the hidden size, as `rope_bwd` did for the 7B).
**The vocabulary** **[measured]**: 248,320 = 97 chunks of 2,560 columns (2,560 = 128 x 20: **no padding**, unlike 152,064); `ce_stats`, `ce_comb`, `ce_grad` accepted on both targets at 512 and 2,048 rows (`bc 2560 nq 97`); token ids and 1-based columns are exact in f32 (248,320 < 2^24). The 512 MiB limits **[read: `resident.md` 20, 24.3]**: the embedding is a host lookup, a step's rows are an f16 table of 5.2 / 10.5 / 21 MB at 1 / 2 / 4 x 512 (a 512 MiB table holds 100 / 50 / 24 steps: a run is sessions of that many steps, as in 22.1); the 4-bit head is resident files of 636 MB of codes (above the pool: uploaded in 128 MiB chunks), 79 MB of scales; the dequantised f16 head would be 2.54 GB (a program output has no such cap: the 7B's logits were 2.49 GB), so it is dequantised in four slabs of 0.64 GB; the logits are 0.51 / 1.02 / 2.03 GB in f32 and their gradient half that. The head product is 5.2 TFLOP forward at 4 x 512 (0.48 s) and the same for the input gradient: 5% of the step.

### 9.5 How correctness could be established when float32 transformers cannot hold the model
A float32 27B is 108 GB; the dequantised bytes are the base the run uses, so the reference must see the same values.
1. **A tiny random-init `Qwen3_5` of the same layer structure (recommended first; built and used above).** `Qwen3_5Config` with 4 layers (3 linear, 1 full), hidden 24, vocabulary 64, builds locally in a second, in float64 through `hf64.py`; the GDN layer's gradients inside it equal the specification's to 1.9e-14 (9.2). The same model is the end-to-end gradient check of the future session (all LoRA gradients against torch float64 autograd, cosine and relative error, and finite differences of the specification as `tests/qwen.py` does) and runs on the CPU interpreter in the gate. It cannot say anything about real activations.
2. **Real weights, one layer at a time (recommended second).** Dequantise a layer from the Ollama blobs to float32 (1.5 GB for 383 M weights), build `Qwen3_5DecoderLayer` from the config, stream the real hidden state through the 64 layers on the CPU or MPS, saving each layer's input to disk (0.67 GB a 512-token sequence); the backward is the same walk in reverse with `torch.autograd` on one layer at a time from the loss gradient of the head (the head in float32 is 5 GB, its logits 0.5 GB). Peak memory about 12 GB, a few minutes **[estimate]**. This gives, per layer, the residual stream and every LoRA gradient on real inputs, the f16 range of every kept tensor (`range_check.py`'s job at 7B), and the activation statistics 9.2 lacks (key correlation, state norms).
3. **The inference runner's golden log-probabilities** (`scripts/qwen35-check.sh`, three prompts, the fixture of the reference implementation): a forward-only check of the base weights, layer structure and the dequantisation, cheap, to be run first on the first working forward.
4. **A smaller real model.** `~/.ollama/models` holds two real Qwen3.5 models of this architecture **[measured: GGUF headers read, `general.architecture qwen35`]**: Qwen3.5-**4B** (32 layers, 8 full attention, hidden 2,560, SwiGLU 9,216, 16 query heads over 4 of 256, 32 value / 16 key heads, 2.65 GB) and **9B** (hidden 4,096, SwiGLU 12,288, 5.63 GB), both `Q4_K_M` (Q4_K, Q6_K, Q8_0 tensors) with the tokenizer of the 27B (vocabulary 248,320). No Qwen3.8 below 27B exists in any local cache (the Hugging Face cache holds Qwen2.5 0.5B / 1.5B / 7B, the 27B config, whisper, laya). **The 4B is the useful one**: float32 is 17 GB, so transformers can hold it on this machine and a whole-model gradient check on real weights against torch autograd is possible, which the 27B never allows. Costs: a Q4_K / Q6_K dequantiser on the host (the repository reads GGUF: `src/gguf.cho`), a mapping of llama.cpp's tensor names and of whatever reordering its converter applied to the delta-net tensors (`ssm_a` is stored as `-exp(A_log)`, the conv as [4, 8192]) **[open]**, and a requantisation to NVFP4 by `nvfp4.py` (the 1.5B route) if the session is to read 4-bit.
**Recommendation: 1, then 3 and 2 together at the first 27B forward, and 4 as the whole-model real-weights check if its mapping turns out cheap.** (1) gates the kernels, (3) the loader, (2) the numbers a run is judged by.

### 9.6 Relevance, honestly
**On this machine the only competitor is MLX.** torch on MPS cannot run bitsandbytes, and a float16 27B is 54 GB of weights before an activation (the 7B torch baseline needed checkpointing to fit at 27 GB, `resident.md` 28.5); torch on the CPU is out of the question. mlx 0.31.2 and mlx-lm 0.31.3 are installed. What was read and measured:
* **mlx-lm supports the architecture and trains it, slowly** **[read]**. `models/qwen3_5.py` has the gated delta net; `linear_to_lora_layers` (`tuner/utils.py`) puts LoRA on every `nn.Linear` or `QuantizedLinear` of the last `num_layers` layers (`-1` for all), including the delta net's projections, with QLoRA over `nn.quantize`; and `mx.quantize` has the `nvfp4` mode with a global scale **[measured: it runs on random weights; not tried on these blobs]**, so MLX has this format too. **The delta rule in training is a Python loop over tokens**: `use_kernel=not self.training` selects `gated_delta_ops`, one `mx.compile`d step a token (its Metal kernel is the inference path and has no gradient).
* **A tiny random-init model through that path** **[measured: `examples/qwen35/mlx_tiny.py`; 4 layers (3 gated-delta with the real 48 / 16 / 128 / 128 geometry, 1 attention), hidden 512, vocabulary 2,048, rank 16, AdamW, 1 sequence]**:

| MLX path | 128 tokens | 256 | 512 | peak at 512 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mlx-lm token loop | 127 ms, 3.91 GB | 319 ms, 7.22 GB | **934 ms** (548 tokens/s) | **13.9 GB** (9.8 with `grad_checkpoint`, 1,095 ms) |
| the same with a 40-line **chunked port in MLX ops** (`mlx_chunked.py`: HF's algorithm, `mx.linalg.inv` for the solve with its gradient attached by `mx.custom_function`, since MLX 0.31.2 has none; equal to mlx-lm's op version to 2.4e-7 and in gradients to 2e-6) | 23 ms | 43 ms | **87 ms** (5,855 tokens/s) | **2.11 GB** (8 layers: 176 ms, 3.21 GB; 262 ms, 1.88 GB checkpointed) |
The token loop's memory is linear in T: 26 MB a token for 3 layers, **about 213 GB for 48 layers at 512 tokens**: unusable without per-layer checkpointing. The chunked port is 10.7x faster and 6.6x smaller; a user would write it in an afternoon (`hf_check.py` is the oracle). Per gated-delta layer at 512 tokens: **22 ms and 0.275 GB** (difference of 8 and 4 layers).
* **MLX's dense products are faster than ours** **[measured: `mx.matmul` and `mx.quantized_matmul`, 2,048 x 5,120 x 17,408]**: float16 14.9 TFLOPS; 4-bit (g64, fused dequantisation, no scratch) forward 14.1, the input-gradient form (`transpose=False`) 13.9. cancho-gpu: 10.8 all-in on the 7B step, 13.0 for its best single product (`resident.md` 20, 28.5). Dense products are 88% of our 27B step.
* **Extrapolated to the 27B at 1 x 512 [estimate: the dense part at 14.5 TFLOPS, the core at 22 ms x 48 layers, the 2x activation ratio measured on the tiny model]**: MLX about 4.7 s (108 tokens/s, about 36 GB) without checkpointing, about 6.8 s (76 tokens/s) with; cancho-gpu 5.5 s (94 tokens/s, 29.4 GB) and 8.1 s (64 tokens/s, 20.8 GB). The saved activations of the tiny model are 2.0x cancho-gpu's accounting for the same dimensions (0.275 against 0.139 GB a layer): a hand-written backward keeps what it needs and MLX's autodiff keeps its f32 intermediates. **Speed: parity within the uncertainty, MLX probably ahead.** **Memory: cancho-gpu about 2x smaller on activations** *[both corrected 2026-11-04 by the measurement note in 9.4: MLX's chunked port measured 6.26 s (82 tokens/s) and 44.8 GB without checkpointing, 8.18 s (63 tokens/s) and 19.4 GB with; mlx-lm's own token loop does not run without checkpointing and takes 20.5 s with; activations 3.2x, not 2x, cancho-gpu's accounting; speed is not "MLX probably ahead" against the estimates]* , which is the difference between 2 x 512 fitting (39.6 GB) and not (about 56 GB for MLX).
* **CUDA** **[read: unverified]**: torch + flash-linear-attention's Triton kernels (`chunk_gated_delta_rule` with a backward, which `modeling_qwen3_5.py` imports when installed and otherwise replaces by the torch fallback read above), bitsandbytes 4-bit and peft/Unsloth are the established stack and will be faster than anything here; nothing of cancho-gpu has run on NVIDIA (`roadmap` 4), so no claim is made.

**What cancho-gpu would offer that they do not** (specific, and skeptical about each):
1. **The activation memory of a hand-written backward** (about 2x less than MLX autodiff, tiny-model measurement, extrapolated): the only measured advantage, and real only between 1 x 512 and 2 x 512.
2. **The gated-delta core faster than MLX's chunked ops** (about 8 against 22 ms a layer at 512 tokens, **[estimate]**: the forward launches are measured, the backward is not built): 2.7x on 7% of the step. Not decisive. A tiny share of what a user feels.
3. **An exact, checkable pipeline**: a binary64 specification equal to HF's code to 1e-14, per-kernel numpy checks with mutants in the gate, goldens, linear-discipline and bounds checks by the compiler (it refused a `[-3, ...]` window and a 57 KB tile before anything ran), the same bytes as the shipped weights with no requantisation. MLX has none of this by construction; torch + FLA has numerics tests of its own.
4. **One source for Metal and CUDA** (the 7B session already emits both; CUDA has never run). A claim about the compiler, not the model.
5. **A real test of the language**: 27B, 64 layers, a recurrent layer type, 248k vocabulary, 4-bit, in kernels the compiler checks. The probes above found three things worth knowing (`check` missing a staging scratch, fragments that cannot be read back, no triangular solve).

**What it would not offer**: more tokens a second on this machine than MLX (the dense products are 1.4x slower per FLOP, and the margin on the delta core is 7% of the step); a model zoo, a chat template, long contexts (the attention materialises `[t,t]` scores; 512 here, 1,024 at most), the vision tower or the MTP loss; any CUDA result; maturity (MLX's training path exists today and its 27B step can be measured in an hour from the cached 4-bit model, which this work did not do; done 2026-11-04, see the measurement note in 9.4).

### 9.7 Milestones, each ending at a number (exit criteria fixed before building)
* **M10.1 The gated-delta layer in `.chx`, forward and backward, at the real geometry.** The launches of 9.3 (kkt, decay, solve, u/w, Aqk, scan a/bo/bu, their reverse, conv, gates, l2 norms, gated norm) over a random layer with the real shapes (4 x 512, 48 value heads, hidden 5,120, frozen f16 weights). Exit: **every gradient (dx and the layer's parameter gradients) at cosine at least 0.999 and the forward at relative rms at most 2e-3 against `gdn_ref.py`**; and **the core's forward + backward at most 40 ms a layer at 4 x 512** (estimated 32; 14 forward measured). Kill: above 60 ms.
* **M10.2 A tiny random-init Qwen3_5, end to end with LoRA.** 4 layers (3 gated-delta, 1 attention with its sigmoid gate, q/k norm and partial RoPE, which needs `qwen_ref.py` extended), real head geometry, vocabulary 2,048, a 4-bit base: the session's loss, arg max and **all adapter gradients at cosine at least 0.995 against torch float64 autograd on the same weights** (`hf_check.py`'s model), and a 40-step run within 1e-3 of peft in loss. CPU interpreter in the gate, Metal by hand.
* **M10.3 The 27B forward on the real bytes.** The 64 layers on the NVFP4 blobs with the adapters at zero, 1 x 512: the golden log-probabilities of the inference runner (top five, gaps within 0.02, the fixture's tolerance), the **residual stream after every layer within 3e-3 relative L2 of the streamed float32 reference of 9.5(2)**, the f16 range reported (largest value of every kept tensor against 65,504), and **the forward's milliseconds and peak GB** (estimated 2.6 s: 2.4 s of products and 0.17 s of gated-delta core; the training session's buffers are 29 GB).
* **M10.4 A 27B QLoRA step.** 1 x 512 with recompute: the gradients of 8 adapters (layers 0, 3, 31, 63; one of each kind) against the streamed reference at cosine at least 0.99, 20 steps with a falling loss, **step at most 9 s and at most 22 GB**; the same task timed in MLX from the cached model the same day.

### 9.8 Verdict: GO-WITH-CONDITIONS
Feasible, in kernels the surface already expresses; the cost is launches, not a missing feature: no compiler change is required for the plan above. **Conditions**: (1) it is justified as a correctness, memory and language-reach milestone, **not** as a speed or "beats MLX" one: the measured facts above say the dense part will be about 1.4x slower than MLX's and the delta core is a small part of the step; (2) **measure MLX's 27B QLoRA step from the cached 4-bit model before M10.3**, so that the bar is a number on this machine; (3) M10.1's kill number (60 ms a layer) and a dense rate under 9 TFLOPS on the 27B shapes are each a stop; (4) the first version uses the 3-launch scan and the register forward substitution; no `matmul` chains.
**Blocking unknowns, ranked**:
1. **The backward's speed and the f16 accuracy of its gradients on real activations** (only the forward launches are timed, on zeros; the f16 error is emulated on synthetic keys). M10.1.
2. **What MLX does on the 27B on this machine** (extrapolated from a tiny model; its dense rate is measured, its delta layer and activation memory are not at scale).
3. **The dense rate at these shapes** (10.8 TFLOPS is the 7B's; 1,024- and 512-row products and the 248k-wide head are untested; 128 x 128 register tiles fall to 0.6 to 1.7 TFLOPS, `resident.md` 20, which is the compiler-side reason for the gap to MLX's 14.9).
4. **Memory at the margin**: the accounting is calibrated on one run with one fitted constant; the 1.6 GB is unexplained; Metal's default limit is about 48 GB, so 2 x 512 without recompute (39.6 GB) leaves 8 GB; recompute is a writer-level duplication of forward programs, not built.
5. **The f16 range of the 27B** (the 7B's largest kept value was 9.3% of 65,504; nothing is known of this model's massive activations; the residual stream is f32 but the SwiGLU output is an f16 operand).
6. **Which reference to trust** (9.5): the streamed float32 layer walk is cheap to build but unbuilt; the 4B route needs a name and layout mapping.
7. **Session scale**: about 5,000 programs for 64 layers (the 7B had 2,053) and the compile time of its distinct kernels; the dequantised weights' scratch aliasing across 64 layers; the 24-step table of a 4 x 512 session.
8. **The base's own loss** (what NVFP4 costs this model against bf16, whose original is not in the cache).

### 9.9 Limits of this section
One machine; every 27B number is arithmetic on a model calibrated on the 7B run and on forward probes, except the manifest bytes, the dense MLX rates, the probe times and the tiny-model MLX runs; the probe times are single launches alone with zero inputs (the shim's profile adds up to 60 us to small kernels); the backward launches, the full-attention layer's specification (gate, q/k norm, partial RoPE, `(1 + w)` norms) and the session writer for the architecture are not written; the f16 error is measured on synthetic data; nothing ran on CUDA; no 27B weight was read except the manifest and the config.
